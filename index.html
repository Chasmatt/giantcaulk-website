What we do

Commercial caulking is the flexible seal that keeps water and air out of a building envelope. Sealant joints are the weakest link in that envelope and the first thing to fail. A mid-rise moves measurably in every direction with thermal cycling, wind load, and structural settlement, and every joint in the façade has to absorb that movement without losing adhesion.

When a joint fails, water gets into the wall assembly — and the damage shows up in insulation, framing, and interior finishes long before anyone traces it back to a quarter-inch bead of sealant.

We install to spec. Joint cleaning and surface prep to the manufacturer's requirements, primer and closed-cell backer rod sized to a proper 2:1 width-to-depth ratio, then the specified sealant — silicone, polyurethane, or STPE — tooled to a concave profile that bonds two-sided and stretches as designed. We work off submittals, pull adhesion samples where required, and keep the documentation warranty claims depend on.

Crews are trained and equipped for swing stage, boom lift, and rope access work, and we schedule around occupied buildings — nights, weekends, and phased sections when tenant disruption is the constraint.

Why joints fail

Most of the restoration work we're called into isn't a product failure. It's a prep failure. If you recognise more than one of these on your building, the sealant is not the problem.

  1. New sealant applied over old sealant instead of full removal
  2. Inadequate surface prep, or a contaminated substrate
  3. Wrong sealant chemistry for the substrate
  4. No backer rod, or the joint filled too deep — three-sided adhesion
  5. Joint cut too narrow for the movement it has to absorb
  6. Wrong product for the UV or traffic exposure
